\newcommand\editSignificance{
Collections of partially observed nonlinear stochastic dynamic systems arise in diverse scientific applications and raise methodologically challenging statistical inference problems.
We considered this task in the context of analyzing panel time series data collected from ecological studies.
We developed and demonstrated data analysis techniques that apply to a flexible model class, permitting scientists to obtain statistically efficient inference on models describing scientifically plausible mechanisms.
For our experiment, a model with stochastic dynamics provided simpler interpretation than previous determinstic models: variation between experimental replicates was adequately described by dynamic noise rather than systematic differences between replicates.
}

\newcommand\editMPIF{
All likelihood maximization in this article uses the marginalized variant of panel iterated filtering (MPIF; Fig.~\ref{fig:pif}; Algorithm~S1), rather than the panel iterated filter of \citet{breto20} in its original form.
Both algorithms extend iterated filtering \citep{ionides15} to PanelPOMP models by associating a perturbed parameter value with each particle $j$ and using particle-filter resampling to concentrate the parameter swarm around the maximum likelihood estimate; they differ only in the parameter resampling step.
When the swarm is filtered through the data of unit $u$, the algorithm of \citet{breto20} resamples not only the shared parameters $\phi$ and the focal unit's parameters $\psi_u$ but also the parameters $\psi_{\tilde u}$ of every other unit $\tilde u \neq u$, even though the data for unit $u$ are uninformative about $\psi_{\tilde u}$.
The marginalized variant instead resamples only $\phi$ and $\psi_u$, leaving each $\psi_{\tilde u}$ unchanged while filtering through unit $u$.
MPIF and PIF become different only in the presence of unit-specific parameters; they are identical when all parameters are shared between all units.
We adopt MPIF here because \citet{wheeler25} show that it has favorable theoretical and practical properties.}

\newcommand\editAbstractScience{Scientifically, we found that mesocosms in a treatment group behave as stochastic replicates: across all treatment-by-species combinations considered, a model with parameters shared across replicate mesocosms is preferred over models with mesocosm-specific parameters under both AIC and a parametric bootstrap likelihood ratio test. Thus, stochastic models can provide a parsimonious explanation of the observed variation between replicates.}

\newcommand\editParametricBootstrap{A simulation study showed that the chi-square approximation to the likelihood ratio test, supported by Wilks' Theorem, is not accurate in this situation (Section~S4).
To better understand this, we present a simple toy model caricaturing the task of testing for shared parameters in a PanelPOMP model.
In this toy model (Section~S5), it is apparent that the regularity conditions for Wilks' Theorem fail.
We therefore test using a simulation-based distribution for the likelihood ratio statistic at the MLE.
AIC is justified by an asymptotic quadratic approximation to the log-likelihood \citep{aic74} similar to Wilks' Theorem, and so these results also bring into question the accuracy of AIC for model selection.
Large discrepancies of AIC are plausibly resistant to violations of asymptotic justifications.
Small discrepancies should not be considered strong evidence for model selection.}
